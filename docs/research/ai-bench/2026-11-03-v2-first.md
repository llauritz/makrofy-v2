## Takeaways

Written 3 Nov 2026 after reading the numbers. The Setup line says "uncommitted changes" because the food table and four spec fixes were committed after the run (856f934); the code that ran is b5811cc plus those files.

**What the numbers show**

1. **Every two-step pipeline halves the cost of today's fill**, from $14.19 to between $4.70 and $6.73 per 1,000 fills, and the interval excludes zero for all of them. Today's fill searches 71% of staples and 64% of vague input (84 wasted searches); the cascade and the table variants waste none and miss no brand.
2. **#135's cascade against today (the price review's question):** −$7.51 per 1,000 fills (−$9.02 to −$5.92), kcal within tolerance −4 points (−10 to +1, not a clear difference on 57 foods), right kind of reply +1 point. Brands are as good (33/33, mean error 0.6% against 1.0%). The loss is on staples, 78/87 against 83/87. That is Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite's own knowledge without search: firm tofu at 83 kcal per 100 g instead of USDA's 144, half an avocado at 120 instead of 161, plain nonfat Greek yogurt at 98 instead of 89, and scrambled eggs with butter at 250 instead of 215. Today's fill got those right only because it searched.
3. **The food table fixes exactly those staples.** `table-jev` and `jev-table` get 118/120 (mean kcal error 0.5%) at $6.36–6.44, and answer a table staple in 0.22–0.45 s. In 82 table answers none was outside tolerance, and Jev answered nothing from the table that wasn't a staple: it said "none" for every brand that shared a word with a table food (Chobani, KFC, Starbucks, Milka) and for mixed dishes (eggs with butter, oats with milk, medialuna).
4. **Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite in the cascade** is the cheapest ($4.70) and the best on staples without a table (87/87), but asked a question instead of answering on Snickers twice and Chobani once (brands 30/33, right kind of reply 93% on brands). 3.1 with today's single call costs $10.42 and still wastes 60 searches.
5. **Jev adds little on top of the cascade**: `jev-cascade` is within a few cents of `cascade` and no more accurate. Jev's router sends 10 vague fills (empanada, milanesa) down the staple path; that does no harm here, because the table says "none" and the cascade asks. Jev's value in this run is the table match, not the routing.
6. All variants are stable across rounds (kcal spread 0–0.2%). Imperial units still appear in `servingText` (8 for today, 1–2 for the table and cascade variants).

**What they don't show**

- **The table result is partly circular.** Staple references are USDA values and the table is USDA, so a table hit matches the reference by construction. What the run does test is whether Jev picks the right food and portion ("an apple", "half an avocado", "2 Eier, gekocht", "300 ml de leche entera"): 82 of 82 right. It doesn't test how often real users log foods the table holds. 29 of the 31 staples here are table foods, because I wrote both lists.
- 57 foods is small: differences of a few points on staples are inside the noise (see the intervals).
- Vague input has no reference, so only the kind of reply is scored.
- Latency is from Node on L's Mac in Argentina to Vertex `global`, not from the app.

**What to test next**

1. A held-out staple set: 30 common foods not in the table spec, with references from another source (label values, BLS or the Argentine table), to measure false table matches and the cascade's staple accuracy on its own.
2. `table-jev:model=gemini-3.1-flash-lite`: the table for staples, 3.1's cascade for the rest. Check whether 3.1's brand questions are a prompt issue (SEARCH_PROMPT tells it to ask) or the model.
3. Coverage: the share of real fills in the fill log that the table would answer, and which foods to add next. The table should grow from the log, not from my guesses.
4. Two-food sums from the table (eggs with butter, oats with milk), which Jev currently sends to "none".
5. Brands are now nearly all of the cost (~$23 per 1,000 brand fills): a shared answer cache and an Open Food Facts lookup are the next levers.
